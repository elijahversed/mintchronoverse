Spider-Man has had a game on nearly every major platform since the early 1980s, ranging from forgettable film tie-ins to genuinely franchise-defining titles. This section separates the two, and groups everything by studio and era so the good stuff doesn't get buried under decades of licensed-game filler.

## The modern benchmark

<Card title="Insomniac's Spider-Man trilogy" icon="spider" href="#">
  Marvel's Spider-Man (2018), Miles Morales (2020), and Marvel's Spider-Man 2 (2023) are widely regarded as having redefined web-swinging traversal for the genre, and established an original continuity distinct from any comic or film version — its own take on the character rather than an adaptation of one.
</Card>

Insomniac's approach — an experienced Peter Parker already years into being Spider-Man, rather than another origin retread — was a notable departure from how most prior adaptations had approached the character, and it's a big part of why the trilogy is treated as its own continuity rather than a tie-in to anything else.

## Earlier eras worth knowing about

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="8-bit and 16-bit era">
    Early console and computer titles, largely simple action or platforming games, often tied to whatever comic storyline or film was current at release.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="PS1/N64 era">
    The jump to 3D brought web-swinging as a core mechanic for the first time, laying groundwork later titles would build on substantially.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Film tie-in era (2002–2014)">
    A run of games released alongside the Raimi and Amazing Spider-Man films, with wildly varying quality — some are genuinely well-regarded, most exist primarily as marketing.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
