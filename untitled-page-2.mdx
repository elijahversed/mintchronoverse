Spider-Man started here, and the comics remain the deepest, longest-running, and most internally contradictory part of his history — which is exactly what makes them worth organizing properly instead of glossing over.

## Where it started

Spider-Man debuted in _Amazing Fantasy_ #15 in August 1962, created by Stan Lee and Steve Ditko. The premise was almost aggressively unglamorous for a superhero comic at the time: a socially awkward, financially struggling teenager gains powers, makes a selfish choice, and pays for it immediately and permanently. That tension — extraordinary ability paired with ordinary, often losing, personal stakes — is the throughline that every subsequent era keeps returning to, even as the specifics around it change dramatically.

<Note>
  If a version of Spider-Man you're reading about seems to be missing that tension entirely, that's usually a deliberate choice by that era's creative team, not an oversight — worth noting, not assuming.
</Note>

## Broad eras, at a glance

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Silver Age (1962–early 1970s)">
    The Lee/Ditko and later Lee/Romita Sr. era. Establishes Peter Parker, Aunt May, the Daily Bugle, and most of the classic rogues' gallery — Green Goblin, Doctor Octopus, Vulture, Sandman, and more, in rapid succession.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Bronze Age (early 1970s–mid 1980s)">
    A notably darker turn, including storylines that pushed superhero comics toward more mature subject matter than had been typical. Character relationships deepen and consequences start sticking.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Modern Age (mid 1980s–2000s)">
    Includes some of the highest-profile status-quo shifts in the character's history — marriages, deaths, clone storylines, and symbiote-driven villains entering the picture.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Contemporary (2000s–present)">
    Multiple ongoing series running in parallel, the introduction of the Ultimate Universe and Miles Morales, and the Spider-Verse concept tying alternate-reality Spider-People together explicitly.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## A note on volume

Spider-Man has been published across many ongoing series, miniseries, and one-shots simultaneously for most of his history — there isn't a single linear reading order the way there might be for a more contained property. Entries in this section are grouped by era and, within that, by the specific run or event, so you can navigate by "the thing I actually want to read about" rather than by publication date alone.
