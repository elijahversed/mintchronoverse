Groups, tags, nesting, and how entries relate to each other.

Chronoverse's structure is deliberately simple — three ideas cover almost everything.

## Categories

Every entry belongs to exactly one top-level category: **Comics**, **Films & TV**, **Games**, or **Characters**. This is the broadest sort — it answers "what kind of thing is this?" before anything else.

## Groups

Within a category, entries are organized into groups — usually by era, series, or studio. Games, for instance, aren't one long undifferentiated list; they're grouped so that a trilogy or a studio's run of titles sits together, the way you'd actually think about them.

## Nesting

Some entries have natural sub-pages — a game with major DLC, a comic run with a pivotal sub-arc worth its own page. Where that's the case, you'll see the sub-entry nested directly under its parent in the sidebar, rather than floating as an unrelated top-level page.

## Tags

Tags are the fine-grained layer — platform, creative team, continuity, tone, whatever's actually relevant to that specific entry. They don't replace groups; they supplement them. Two entries in completely different groups might still share a tag (`insomniac`, `earth-616`, `origin-story`) and that shared tag is often the fastest way to find related material search alone wouldn't surface.

## Relationships between entries

Most entries end with a short set of related links — sequels, adaptations, the character pages tied to that story. These aren't automatically generated; they're chosen because they're genuinely the next thing worth reading, not just anything that shares a tag.

<Tip>
  The fastest way to go deep on a topic isn't the search bar — it's following these related links two or three entries deep. That's usually how the most interesting connections in Spider-Man media reveal themselves.
</Tip>
