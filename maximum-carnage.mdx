Fourteen parts, five ongoing titles, one summer, and a body count higher than almost anything Spider-Man had been involved in up to that point. Maximum Carnage is the story most responsible for turning Carnage from "a scarier Venom" into one of Marvel's most recognizable villains in his own right — and it's about as pure a distillation of early-90s superhero comics as exists on a printed page.

## What Happens?

Cletus Kasady escapes Ravencroft Asylum when his dormant Carnage symbiote reawakens during a blood test, allowing him to slaughter several guards and staff members. He frees fellow inmate Shriek, and together they escape Ravencroft, eventually recruiting the Doppelganger and forming the beginnings of their own twisted “family.”

Meanwhile, Peter Parker attends Harry Osborn’s funeral alongside Mary Jane and many of his friends and family. His attempt to take a break from being Spider-Man is quickly cut short when he learns of Carnage’s escape. While investigating, Spider-Man encounters Shriek and the Doppelganger but is defeated and knocked from a building.

The chapter ends with Carnage confronting J. Jonah Jameson at the Daily Bugle. Rather than attacking him, Carnage demands Jameson’s help in finding Venom.

## Why it matters

Carnage’s escape from Ravencroft sets the events of the story in motion and marks the beginning of the threat surrounding the newly formed “family.” His alliance with Shriek and the recruitment of the Doppelganger establish the core group of villains Spider-Man will soon have to face.

The chapter also immediately disrupts Peter Parker’s attempt to step away from being Spider-Man, showing that he cannot easily separate his personal life from his responsibilities as a hero. Finally, Carnage’s demand for J. Jonah Jameson to help him find Venom begins the connection between Carnage and Venom, setting up an even larger conflict.
