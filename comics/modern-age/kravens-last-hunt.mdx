Most Spider-Man stories are, structurally, about Peter. This one is really about Kraven the Hunter — a Silver Age villain largely treated as a recurring gimmick fighter up to this point — and it uses Spider-Man almost as a supporting figure in Kraven's own story.

## What happens

Convinced that defeating Spider-Man through combat alone would prove nothing about his own worth, Kraven shoots and buries Spider-Man alive, then dons the costume himself to patrol as a version of the hero he considers more effective — brutal where Peter is restrained. The arc plays out as much as a psychological character study of Kraven as it does a rescue story once Peter claws his way back.

<Note>
  The story ends with Kraven taking his own life, having achieved what he set out to prove and finding no lasting satisfaction in it — a notably bleak, literary conclusion for a mainstream superhero comic of its era.
</Note>

## Why it matters

_Kraven's Last Hunt_ is consistently ranked among the best Spider-Man stories ever published, largely because it does something the character's history rarely attempts: it centers a villain's interiority as the actual subject of the story, using Spider-Man's absence for most of it to make the point. It also permanently reframed Kraven from a recurring nuisance into one of the character's most respected antagonists.
