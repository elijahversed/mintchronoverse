Two issues, one confrontation with the Green Goblin, and an outcome that superhero comics — where the hero's loved ones were generally protected by narrative convention — hadn't really done before.

## What happens

The Green Goblin, having deduced Spider-Man's identity, targets Gwen Stacy, Peter's girlfriend, directly. In the confrontation that follows, Gwen falls from a bridge; Spider-Man's attempt to save her comes at a fatal cost regardless. The second issue deals with the immediate aftermath and Peter's confrontation with the Goblin, which ends with the Goblin's own death.

<Note>
  These two issues are frequently discussed as the moment mainstream superhero comics shifted from Silver Age optimism — where the hero generally wins cleanly — to a Bronze Age willingness to let a story's stakes actually cost the protagonist something permanent.
</Note>

## Why it matters

Gwen Stacy's death is one of the most referenced single events in superhero comics history, cited constantly in discussions of when and how mainstream comics started taking narrative risk seriously. It permanently altered Peter Parker's supporting cast and emotional throughline, and later stories — including a well-known Ultimate Universe divergence — have returned to reinterpret or directly reference it.
