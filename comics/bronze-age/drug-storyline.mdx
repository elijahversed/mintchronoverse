<Info>
  **Published:** May–July 1971 · **Writer:** Stan Lee · **Artist:** Gil Kane · **Era:** Bronze Age
</Info>

The Comics Code Authority — the industry's self-censorship body — explicitly prohibited depicting drug use in any context, even negatively. When the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services asked Marvel to do a story addressing drug abuse anyway, Stan Lee wrote one, submitted it, and Marvel published all three issues without the Code's seal after it was refused.

## What happens

A friend of Peter's is shown using pills, spirals into dependency, and Peter — as Spider-Man — is unable to simply fight the problem away, since it isn't the kind of threat his powers are built to solve. The story treats addiction as a consequence of circumstance rather than a moral failing, which was itself a notable stance for the time.

<Warning>
  This entry describes the storyline's subject matter for historical context. It isn't a detailed account of drug use or its effects, and shouldn't be read as one.
</Warning>

## Why it matters

The public backing Marvel received for defying the Code over this story is widely credited as a turning point that led the Code to loosen its rules shortly afterward, making more socially grounded storytelling possible across the industry, not just at Marvel. It's also an early, clear example of the series using Spider-Man's powerlessness against certain kinds of problems as the actual point of a story, rather than a flaw to be written around.
