_Amazing Fantasy_ was already on its way out. Marvel had decided to cancel the low-selling anthology series, and its final issue became, almost by accident, one of the most consequential single issues in comics history.

## What happens

Peter Parker is a shy, unassuming high-school student who prefers the company of his teachers to that of his classmates, who regard him as Midtown High's resident wallflower. His only living relatives are his Uncle Ben and Aunt May, who are deeply fond of him.

While attending a demonstration of radioactivity at a science hall, Peter is bitten by a spider that has accidentally passed through a radioactive ray. The spider dies shortly afterward, while Peter begins to feel light-headed and leaves the demonstration.

Outside, Peter is nearly struck by a car. He instinctively leaps out of the way, only to discover that he has jumped much farther than he expected. He lands against the side of a building and realizes that he can cling to its surface. After climbing to the roof, he accidentally crushes a steel pipe in his hand.

Peter concludes that the spider bite has somehow given him extraordinary strength, speed, and climbing abilities. For the first time, he begins thinking about what he could do with his newfound powers.

### Becoming Spider-Man

Peter soon discovers an opportunity to make money from his abilities. He encounters a wrestling advertisement offering \$100 to anyone who can remain in the ring for three minutes against Crusher Hogan.

Peter returns wearing a makeshift disguise and enters the match. Hogan initially mocks him as the "Masked Marvel," but Peter quickly proves that his powers are genuine, lifting the wrestler over his head and carrying him up one of the ring posts.

After the match, a television producer approaches Peter and encourages him to pursue a career in show business. Peter takes the opportunity and begins looking for a way to turn his abilities into a public identity.

At home, he creates a spider-themed costume, develops a supply of sticky web fluid, and builds wrist-mounted devices capable of firing it.

He gives himself a name: **Spider-Man.**

Spider-Man quickly becomes a television celebrity, giving Peter the fame and money he had hoped his powers could provide.

### The burglar

Peter's attitude toward his abilities changes only after a seemingly insignificant encounter backstage at a television studio.

He discovers a security guard chasing a thief. Rather than helping, Peter allows the thief to escape, dismissing the situation as the guard's responsibility.

Peter has no reason to believe that his decision will matter.

Days later, he returns home and learns that a burglar has killed Uncle Ben. The police have cornered the suspect inside a deserted warehouse, and Peter immediately puts on his Spider-Man costume and heads to the scene.

Inside the warehouse, Spider-Man confronts the burglar, crawling down the wall toward him and using his webbing to neutralize him.

When Peter sees the man's face, he recognizes him as the same thief he had deliberately allowed to escape at the television studio.

The connection is immediate.

Peter realizes that if he had stopped the thief when he had the chance, Uncle Ben would still be alive.

### The lesson

Uncle Ben's death forces Peter to confront the consequences of his earlier decision. His powers had given him the ability to help someone, but he had chosen not to because he believed the situation wasn't his responsibility.

That realization becomes the foundation of Peter Parker's life as Spider-Man.

<Note>
  The principle established by the story — that extraordinary power carries an equally extraordinary responsibility — becomes the defining ethic of Spider-Man. It transforms Peter's powers from something he uses for personal gain into something he believes he must use to help others.
</Note>

Peter dedicates himself to fighting crime, beginning his life as Spider-Man.
