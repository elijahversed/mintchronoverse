Introduced as a hired antagonist manipulated into targeting Spider-Man, the Punisher wasn't written as a future flagship character — he was a one-issue foil meant to contrast Spider-Man's restraint against someone willing to kill.

## What happens

Frank Castle is introduced as a former Marine turned vigilante who executes criminals rather than turning them over to the justice system, immediately establishing the core ethical line that would define the character afterward: not villain, not hero, but someone whose methods conflict directly with those of the heroes around him. His first appearance has him deceived into believing Spider-Man is a criminal worth eliminating, only for the truth to come out over the course of the story.

<Tip>
  This contrast — a character who's arguably "on the same side" but achieves it through means the hero can't endorse — became the basis for the Punisher's decades-long career as one of Marvel's most morally contested figures.
</Tip>

## Why it matters

Few one-off antagonists go on to headline their own long-running series, films, and adaptations the way the Punisher has. His debut here is a useful example of how the Spider-Man comics have functioned, throughout their history, as a launching point for characters who outgrew their original story entirely.
