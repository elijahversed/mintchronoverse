A cheat sheet for the shorthand the archive assumes you know. Bookmark this one.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Earth-616">
    The designation for Marvel's primary, ongoing comics continuity — "mainline" Spider-Man. Most other adaptations reference this version even when they diverge from it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Retcon">
    Short for "retroactive continuity" — when a later story changes or reinterprets something established earlier, without a full reboot. Extremely common across sixty years of publishing.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Elseworld / What If">
    A story deliberately told outside normal continuity, exploring an alternate premise without affecting the main line. Consequence-free by design.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Legacy character">
    A codename passed to a new person over time. "Spider-Man" itself has effectively become a legacy identity across some continuities, even though Peter Parker remains the most persistent holder of it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Tie-in">
    A game, comic, or show made to accompany a film's release, generally adapting that film's specific continuity rather than establishing its own.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Reboot">
    A full reset of a continuity's history, as opposed to a retcon's partial adjustment. The Amazing Spider-Man films, for instance, rebooted the film continuity established by the Raimi trilogy.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Spider-Verse">
    The broader concept of the many alternate-reality Spider-People existing in parallel, popularized by the _Spider-Verse_ comics event and later the animated films. Not a single continuity — a framework for how continuities relate to each other.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Original continuity (games)">
    A game built around its own self-contained story rather than adapting an existing comic or film. Insomniac's Marvel's Spider-Man is the clearest modern example.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Canon">
    Whether something is considered part of a continuity's "official" ongoing story. Chronoverse tracks this per-continuity rather than trying to assign one universal canon — see [Canon & Continuity](/essentials/canon-and-continuity).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
