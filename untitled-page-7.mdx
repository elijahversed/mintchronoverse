Every other section of this archive is organized around a _medium_ — comics, film, games. This section is organized around _people_, and it's deliberately the section most likely to send you sideways into the other three.

## Why this section exists separately

A character page doesn't belong to one continuity — it exists to show you the full spread. Peter Parker's comics entry links out to his film appearances, his game appearances, and the specific runs where his character shifted the most. That's the point: this is the connective tissue between everything else in the archive.

## What's on a character page

<Steps>
  <Step title="Core identity">
    Real name, primary continuity, first appearance — the baseline facts that don't change much between versions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="History">
    A summary of how the character has developed, generally focused on their primary continuity unless otherwise noted.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Abilities & equipment">
    What they can actually do — useful context since power sets and gear have shifted meaningfully across eras.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Related">
    Direct links to the specific comics, films, and games where this version of the character actually appears.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  A character page generally centers on one primary continuity for its main write-up (usually Earth-616, where applicable) and links out to variants rather than trying to describe every version in one block of text. If you're looking for a specific film or game version of a character, the related links are the fastest route there.
</Note>

## Beyond the two Spider-Men

Villains, supporting cast, and legacy characters all live here too — Doctor Octopus, Gwen Stacy, Ben Reilly, and the rest of the extended cast that makes the ongoing story worth tracking in the first place. If a name keeps showing up across comics, film, and games and you want the throughline, this is the section that has it.

<Tip>
  Start broad, then follow the related links down into whichever medium interests you most — that's usually a faster path to what you actually want than searching for a specific title.
</Tip>
