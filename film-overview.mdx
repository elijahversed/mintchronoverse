Spider-Man has been rebooted for film more times than almost any other major superhero property — and unlike the comics, where eras blend into each other gradually, each film continuity is a clean, deliberate break from the last.

## Three live-action eras

<Steps>
  <Step title="Raimi Trilogy (2002–2007)">
    Sam Raimi's films, starring Tobey Maguire, are widely credited with proving that superhero films could be massive mainstream box-office successes, paving the way for the genre's dominance in the following decade.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The Amazing Spider-Man (2012–2014)">
    A reboot starring Andrew Garfield, restarting the origin story with a different tone and a different supporting cast emphasis, notably centering Gwen Stacy more heavily than prior adaptations.
  </Step>
  <Step title="MCU Spider-Man (2016–present)">
    Tom Holland's version, integrated directly into the Marvel Cinematic Universe rather than existing as a standalone franchise — a structurally different approach from either prior era.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  These three eras are not connected continuities. Each is a self-contained version of the character, even when plot beats or character names overlap.
</Note>

## Animation

Spider-Man has an extensive animated history separate from any live-action continuity, including multiple standalone series across different decades and tones. The most significant recent addition is the _Spider-Verse_ film series, which took the "many Spider-People across many realities" concept that had existed in the comics and built an entire visual and narrative structure around it — arguably the most direct adaptation of the multiverse idea covered in [Canon & Continuity](/canon-and-continuity).

## Why continuity matters more here than anywhere else

Because film and TV adaptations are typically made with defined start and end points (a trilogy, a series run), they tend to have the cleanest internal continuity of any medium in this archive — no sixty years of accumulated retcons to track. That makes them a genuinely good starting point if the scale of the comics feels intimidating.
