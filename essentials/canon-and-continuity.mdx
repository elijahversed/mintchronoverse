## The problem with "one true timeline"

Spider-Man has been written continuously since 1962 by hundreds of different writers, across comics, films, TV, and games, none of whom were required to agree with each other. Ask three different eras of comics who Spider-Man's first villain was, or how exactly Uncle Ben died, and you'll get three answers that are all "correct" — correct for _that_ story.

Trying to smash all of that into a single consistent timeline doesn't produce clarity. It produces a mess, and it erases the actual, interesting differences between versions. So Chronoverse doesn't do that.

<Note>
  Instead: every entry is tagged by its own continuity. When two entries disagree, that's not an error in the archive — it's the point.
</Note>

## The big buckets

Most Spider-Man media falls into one of a few broad continuity buckets. You'll see these referenced constantly across the archive:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Earth-616 — the main comics universe">
    The primary, ongoing Marvel comics continuity. This is "mainline" Spider-Man — the version most other adaptations borrow from, even when they diverge.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Ultimate Universe (Earth-1610 and successors)">
    A line originally created to let new readers jump in without sixty years of backstory. This is where Miles Morales was introduced as Spider-Man, distinct from Peter Parker's version.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Film continuities">
    Each film series — the Raimi trilogy, the Amazing Spider-Man duology, the MCU version, the Spider-Verse animated films — is its own self-contained continuity, even when they share a studio or a name.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Game continuities">
    Some games adapt an existing continuity (film tie-ins), and some — like Insomniac's Marvel's Spider-Man — establish their own original continuity that isn't quite any of the above.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Elseworlds & alternate realities">
    One-off or limited stories deliberately set outside normal continuity, free to ask "what if" without consequences for the main line.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## How this shows up on a page

Every entry that belongs to a specific continuity says so, usually right in the tags or the opening line. When you see something like _"Earth-616"_ or _"MCU"_ on an entry, that's telling you which version of the story you're reading — useful context, not trivia.

<Tip>
  If you only remember one thing from this page: **"which universe is this?" is always a reasonable question to ask, and the archive is built to let you answer it quickly.**
</Tip>

## Why this matters for you

Once this clicks, a few things stop being confusing:

- Why "Spider-Man's first appearance" can correctly mean three different things depending on what you're reading about.
- Why a character's personality, powers, or backstory might look different across two entries that both seem to be about "the same" character.
- Why the archive links related entries instead of merging them — the relationships matter more than forcing agreement.
